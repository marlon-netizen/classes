Symbols and rights: respect on and off screen

How can we respect our national symbols and everyone's human rights?

Guiding question

"How can we show respect for our national symbols and, at the same time, respect everyone's human rights?"

Learning target: I can judge whether a real or fictional case shows respect for our national symbols and for human rights, argue my position respectfully in a debate, and help create a class "Decalogo del Respeto" that protects those rights at school.

Key ideas

Select each idea to read it. Then try the worked examples.

Real-life connections

  • Civic acts: when a school ceremony honors the flag and anthem, how we behave shows whether we respect the community that shares them.
  • Social media: a post can reach hundreds of people in minutes. It can honor a symbol or hurt people, and it is hard to take back.
  • At school: shared rules, like a Decalogo del Respeto, protect everyone's dignity so that disagreements stay respectful.

Note: rules about how national symbols may be used differ from country to country. Ask your teacher about the rules in your country.

Additional reference

Human Rights Quick Reference

These ideas come from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations, 1948). The summaries are in simple words and are not word-for-word quotes. You can read the full text on the UN website.

ArticleIn simple wordsLink to our topic
Article 1All people are born free and equal in dignity and rights.Everyone's dignity deserves respect, online and offline.
Article 2Rights belong to everyone without discrimination.A symbol must never be used to exclude or mock a group.
Article 12No one should suffer attacks on their privacy, honor or reputation.Do not post humiliating images or comments about others.
Article 19Everyone has the right to hold opinions and to express them.You can share your views, and debate respectfully.
Article 29We have duties to our community, and rights can be limited to protect the rights of others.Freedom of expression comes with responsibility.
