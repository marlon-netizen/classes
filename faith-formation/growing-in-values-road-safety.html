Elements of traffic

Moving Safely in My Community

Guiding question

"How can I protect my life and the lives of others when I move around my community?"

Learning target: I can identify the main elements of traffic and their purpose, explain safe behaviors for moving around my community, and propose one concrete action to improve road safety at home, at school or in my neighborhood.

Road safety is a value

Following traffic rules is not only about avoiding a fine. It shows respect for life and responsibility toward your family, your classmates and everyone who shares the road. Whether you walk, ride in a car or bus, or ride a bike, your choices protect you and the people around you.

Elements of traffic

Select each element to read what it is, what it is for, and the safe behavior that goes with it.

Three ways we move: three sets of safe behaviors

You are a…Safe behaviors
Pedestrian (on foot)Use the crosswalk · wait for the walk signal · look and listen before crossing · no phone while crossing · walk on the sidewalk
Passenger (in a car or bus)Buckle your seatbelt on every ride (back seat too) · stay seated · keep arms and head inside · do not distract the driver · get out on the curb side
Cyclist (on a bike)Wear a helmet that fits · obey signs and signals · use hand signals · be visible with lights or reflectors · do not use a phone

Traffic rules differ from country to country. Ask your teacher about the rules where you live.

"Traffic light of behaviors"

Use colors to judge a situation: green = safe, red = unsafe, yellow = "it depends." When you say "it depends," explain what the safe choice would be, and name the traffic element involved (sign, crosswalk, seatbelt, helmet).

Write it by hand

Notebook Work and Exit Ticket

Get your notebook or a sheet of paper and a pencil. Your answers here are not typed on this page. Write or draw them by hand.

Your Safe Route map

In your notebook, sketch a simple map of a route you often walk, cycle or ride (for example, home to school). Mark at least 2 risk points (for example: no crosswalk, a broken sidewalk, fast traffic) and write the safe action for each one. See Worked Example 5.

My road-safety pledge

Write one concrete action you will practice this week, for example "I will always use the crosswalk on the way to school," "I will always wear my seatbelt," or "I will wear my helmet when I cycle." See Worked Example 6.

Self-check (think, then write one sentence)

  • I can name the traffic element involved in a situation (sign, crosswalk, seatbelt, helmet).
  • I can explain a safe behavior for a pedestrian, a passenger and a cyclist.
  • I took part in my team's case analysis and Safe Route map.
