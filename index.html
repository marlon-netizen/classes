Part A

Efficiency of heat engines

A heat engine absorbs energy as heat from a high-temperature body (Qh), does work on the environment (W), and gives up the rest to a low-temperature body (Qc). By the first law of thermodynamics, the work per cycle is the difference between heat in and heat out:

Wnet = Qnet = Qh − Qc
Second law of thermodynamics No cyclic process that converts heat entirely into work is possible.
So W can never equal Qh in a cycle: some energy must always be expelled to the surroundings, Qc > 0.
Why doesn't the first law already forbid Qc = 0?

The first law only says energy is conserved: what goes in must come out as work or heat. An engine with Qc = 0 would conserve energy perfectly. The requirement to expel energy at a lower temperature is a separate, additional law — the second law.

Efficiency (eff)

Efficiency compares the useful energy taken out of a process (work) to the total energy put in (heat from the hot body):

eff = WnetQh = Qh − QcQh = 1 − QcQh
  • Efficiency is unitless; use only the magnitudes of the energies.
  • eff = 1 (100 %) would require Qc = 0 — impossible. Every real engine has eff < 1.
  • To raise efficiency, increase Qh or reduce Qc, making Qc/Qh smaller.

Part B

Entropy

Shuffle a deck of cards: it's very unlikely they land sorted by suit and number. There is only a few ways to make the highly ordered arrangement, but more than 8 × 1067 ways to arrange 52 cards. Systems left to themselves drift toward the arrangement that can happen in the most ways.

Key term Entropy — a measure of the randomness or disorder of a system. The greater the entropy, the greater the disorder. The entropy of a system tends to increase; once at maximum entropy, it tends to stay there.

Greater disorder means less energy available to do work

If every gas particle in a cylinder moved toward the piston in an orderly way, all of the internal energy could do work. That is extremely improbable: the particles move in every direction and collide with the walls and each other, so not all of the energy is available for useful work.

Gas expansion simulator

Disorder (entropy) of the arrangement
Particles start packed in the left chamber — a highly ordered, low-entropy state.

What to notice

  • Nobody "pushes" the particles to spread out. There are simply far more ways to be spread out than packed in the corner.
  • Wait a while: the particles never spontaneously return to the left chamber. That is the arrow of entropy.
  • Energy is still conserved, but the ordered "all-moving-the-same-way" motion needed for work is gone.
Second law, entropy version The entropy of the universe increases in all natural processes.

Entropy can decrease locally — if it increases more elsewhere

Refrigerator: water freezing into ice loses entropy (a small decrease), but the refrigerator exhausts heat into the room air, which gains more entropy (a large increase). Net result: total entropy goes up.

Did you know?Living things build complex, orderly structures (cells, tissues) — a local entropy decrease. It works because so much energy comes from the Sun that the total entropy of Earth, the Sun, and space still increases.

Real-world connection · Deep-sea air conditioning

Systems in Hawaii pump frigid water from half a mile down the ocean to cool buildings, using a heat exchanger instead of energy-hungry compressors. Because heat naturally flows from the building's warm water to the very cold sea water, only pumps are needed — roughly 10 % of the electricity of conventional systems. A large temperature difference between two bodies is what makes this transfer happen on its own.
