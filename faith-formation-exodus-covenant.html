Exodus and Covenant

The Journey from Slavery to Freedom

What would you risk to be free?

Keep this question in mind as you study. The people of Israel were slaves in Egypt. God led them out, step by step, and gave them a way to live that kept them free.

Standard A.3.6.4 – Jesus is the fulfillment of the Old Covenant and established the New Covenant.
By the end of this page you can: put the Passover, Exodus and Sinai in order · explain what the lamb's blood signified · explain the purpose of the Law · explain how Jesus fulfills the Old Covenant in the New Covenant.

The Virtue of Order

God gives exact instructions to protect His people and lead them to freedom.

Virtue: Order – Following the right rules and sequence keeps us safe and free.

Think about it: What needs an order to work? A recipe, a basketball play, a fire drill. If the steps are skipped or mixed up, things go wrong. The Passover night was like that: God's instructions were exact because lives depended on them.

The New Covenant

Old Covenant

The lamb's blood on the doorposts (Exodus 12) and the covenant at Sinai (Exodus 19–24). God makes Israel His own people and gives them the Law.

New Covenant

Jesus at the Last Supper, during the Passover meal, says the cup is the new covenant in His blood (Luke 22:19–20). Jesus is the Lamb of God (John 1:29).

Fulfillment does not mean "throwing away." Jesus said He came not to abolish the Law but to fulfill it (Matthew 5:17). The Old Covenant points ahead; the New Covenant completes it.

Real-life connections

  • A fire drill: exact instructions save lives, like the instructions of the Passover night.
  • Family traditions: holiday meals that families repeat remind them who they are, as the Passover meal reminded Israel.
  • Sports and teams: rules make the game possible and fair. They do not take away the fun; they make it playable.
  • Promises: a promise or commitment binds you and also frees you to be trusted.
