Complex Numbers

Complex numbers extend the real numbers so that every negative number has a square root. In this lesson you will simplify square roots of negative numbers, find powers of i, and add, subtract, multiply and divide complex numbers.

i = √−1    and    i² = −1 A complex number has the form a + bi, where a is the real part and b is the imaginary part.

1. Simplifying square roots of negative numbers

For a positive number a:

√−a = i√a

Then simplify the radical by pulling out perfect-square factors, e.g. √18 = √9·2 = 3√2.

Watch out: the rule √a·√b = √ab only works when a and b are not negative. Rewrite each square root with i first, then multiply.

2. Powers of i

The powers of i repeat in a cycle of 4:

i¹ = i
i² = −1
i³ = −i
i⁴ = 1

Shortcut: divide the exponent by 4 and use the remainder.

  • Remainder 0 → 1    Remainder 1 → i    Remainder 2 → −1    Remainder 3 → −i

3. Operations with complex numbers

  • Add / subtract: combine the real parts and combine the imaginary parts. (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i. Distribute the minus sign when subtracting.
  • Multiply: use FOIL / distribution, then replace i² with −1.
  • Conjugate: the conjugate of a + bi is a − bi. Their product is a real number: (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b².
  • Divide: multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, then write the result as a + bi.
  • Modulus (absolute value): |a + bi| = √a² + b².

4. Quadratic equations with complex solutions

If the discriminant b² − 4ac is negative, the quadratic formula gives two complex solutions that are conjugates of each other.

x = −b ± √b² − 4ac2a

Worked examples

Example 1 — Simplify √−72

√−72 = i√72 = i√36·2 = 6i√2

Example 2 — Multiply √−6 · √−24

Rewrite with i first: (i√6)(i√24) = i²√144 = (−1)(12) = −12

Example 3 — Simplify i²³

23 ÷ 4 = 5 remainder 3, so i²³ = i³ = −i

Example 4 — Add, subtract and multiply

(6 + 4i) + (−2 + 9i) = 4 + 13i

(6 + 4i) − (−2 + 9i) = 6 + 2 + (4 − 9)i = 8 − 5i

(3 + 2i)(1 − 4i) = 3 − 12i + 2i − 8i² = 3 − 10i + 8 = 11 − 10i

Example 5 — Divide (2 + 3i) ÷ (4 − i)

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 4 + i:

Numerator: (2 + 3i)(4 + i) = 8 + 2i + 12i + 3i² = 5 + 14i

Denominator: (4 − i)(4 + i) = 16 + 1 = 17

Result: 517 + 1417i

Example 6 — Solve x² + 4x + 13 = 0

Discriminant: 4² − 4(1)(13) = 16 − 52 = −36

x = (−4 ± √−36)/2 = (−4 ± 6i)/2 = −2 ± 3i
