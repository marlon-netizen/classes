Part A

Coordinate systems in two dimensions

In one dimension a direction is just a plus or minus sign. In two dimensions we use both the x-axis and the y-axis at the same time. The axes point in fixed directions. For example, the positive y-axis points north and the positive x-axis points east. A jet flying at 300 m/s to the northeast can then be described as v = 300 m/s at 45° above the +x axis.

Axes fixed to north and east: a vector can point into any of the four quadrants.

Tips and tricksThere are no firm rules for choosing axes. As long as you are consistent, the final answer is correct with any system. Choose the one that makes the problem easiest: for objects thrown into the air, put the y-axis along the vertical.
Part B

Finding the magnitude and direction of a resultant

Drawing and measuring takes time and depends on how carefully you draw. When two vectors are perpendicular (for example Δx and Δy), a calculator is faster and more accurate.

Pythagorean theorem (magnitude)
d2 = Δx2 + Δy2  →  d = √(Δx2 + Δy2)
Works for any right triangle: (hypotenuse)2 = (leg)2 + (other leg)2.
Tangent function (direction)
tan θ = oppositeadjacent = ΔyΔx
θ = tan−1(ΔyΔx)
The angle is measured from a reference line, for example "49.8° north of east".

An archaeologist climbs a pyramid: Δx = 115 m (half the width) and Δy = 136 m (the height), so d = 178 m at θ = 49.8°.

Tips and tricksMake sure your calculator is set to degrees (not radians or grads). Use the absolute values of Δx and Δy in the tangent, then describe the direction in words (north/south of east/west).
Part C

Resolving a vector into components

Any vector can be described by a pair of perpendicular components: its projections on the x- and y-axes. The components are scalars with signs (they tell which way along each axis). Resolving a vector lets you study the motion in each direction separately.

Sine and cosine
sin θ = oppositehypotenuse
cos θ = adjacenthypotenuse
Components (θ measured from the x-axis)
Δx = d cos θ    Δy = d sin θ

A highway segment 53.0 km long at 48.7° north of west: Δx = 35.0 km (west), Δy = 39.8 km (north).

Tips and tricksDo not assume that cosine is always for x and sine for y. It depends on where the angle is: the component adjacent to the angle uses cosine, the component opposite uses sine. Check your answer with the Pythagorean theorem: Δx2 + Δy2 must equal d2.

Part D

Adding vectors that are not perpendicular

Until now the vector-addition problems used perpendicular vectors. Many objects, however, move in one direction and then turn at an angle. Suppose a plane first travels 5 km at an angle of 35° to the ground, then climbs at only 10° relative to the ground for 22 km. The two displacement vectors do not form a right triangle, so you cannot apply the tangent function or the Pythagorean theorem to the original vectors. Instead, resolve each vector into components; the sums of the components are the two perpendicular components of the resultant.

For any vectors that are not perpendicular, add their components:

  1. Resolve every vector into x- and y-components (with signs).
  2. Add all the x-components to get Δxtot, and all the y-components to get Δytot.
  3. Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the magnitude of the resultant: d = √(Δxtot2 + Δytot2).
  4. Use tan−1(|Δytot| ÷ |Δxtot|) and the signs of the totals to state the direction (which quadrant).

Two vectors that are not perpendicular, added tip-to-tail; the resultant is the dashed arrow.

Tips and tricksAngles measured clockwise from the positive x-axis (for example "35° south of east") are conventionally negative: θ = −35°. With that convention a calculator gives the correct signs for cos θ and sin θ automatically.
Check your workThe resultant cannot be longer than the sum of the magnitudes of the vectors, and it must point into the quadrant given by the signs of Δxtot and Δytot.

Remember when solving problems

  1. Analyze — draw the vectors on axes; decide which angle is given and from which axis.
  2. Plan — perpendicular vectors: Pythagorean theorem + tangent. Single vector: sine and cosine for components. Several vectors: add components first.
  3. Solve — keep the signs of the components; calculator in degrees; state the direction in words.
  4. Check — d² = Δx² + Δy²; the quadrant matches the signs; the magnitude is not larger than the sum of the magnitudes.
