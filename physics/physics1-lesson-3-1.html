Part A

Scalars and vectors

In "Motion in One Dimension" we described direction with a plus or minus sign. That only works for motion along a straight line. To describe motion that does not follow a straight line we need a way to record a direction in a plane.

ScalarA physical quantity that has magnitude but no direction.
Examples: speed, volume, the number of pages in a book, time.
VectorA physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction.
Examples: displacement, velocity, acceleration.

A pilot planning a trip must know exactly how far and which way to fly: that is a displacement, so it is a vector. To describe the velocity of a bird we must give the speed (say 3.5 m/s) and the direction (say northeast).

QuantityScalar or vector?Example
distance, speed, time, mass, volumescalar3.5 m/s
displacement, velocity, acceleration, forcevector3.5 m/s to the northeast
Part B

Drawing and writing vectors

Quantities are written with symbols, such as t for time. To keep vectors and scalars apart, this course uses boldface type for vectors and italics for scalars:

v = 3.5 m/s  (speed, scalar)     v = 3.5 m/s to the northeast  (velocity, vector)

When you write by hand you cannot use boldface, so draw a small arrow over the symbol: v⃗ = 3.5 m/s to the northeast.

In a diagram a vector is an arrow:

  • the arrow points in the direction of the vector;
  • the length of the arrow is proportional to the magnitude (a vector twice as long is twice as large);
  • the end with the arrowhead is the head (tip) and the other end is the tail.

Three vectors: A = 3 m up, B = 2 m to the right, C = 3 m to the right. A and C have the same magnitude but different directions; B and C have the same direction but different magnitudes.

Same vector?Two arrows represent the same vector only if they have the same magnitude AND the same direction. Where the arrow is drawn on the page does not matter.
Part C

Adding vectors graphically: the resultant

A resultant is a vector that represents the sum of two or more vectors. Only vectors that describe the same kind of quantity and have the same units can be added: you cannot add a velocity to a displacement, or meters to feet.

Think of a student who walks 1600 m to a friend's house and then 1600 m to school. The direct path from his house to the school is the vector sum of the two displacements. To find it by drawing:

  1. Choose a reasonable scale (for example 50 m = 1 cm on paper).
  2. Draw the first vector with the right direction and scaled length.
  3. Draw the second vector with its tail at the head of the first, again with the right direction and scaled length.
  4. Draw the resultant from the tail of the first to the head of the last.
  5. Measure its length with a ruler and multiply by the scale to get the magnitude. Measure the angle with a protractor to get the direction.

Tip-to-tail (triangle) method: (a) and (b) are the two displacements, the dashed arrow (c) is their resultant.

TipsAlways label every arrow, write the scale, and state the direction of the resultant relative to a reference line (for example "10° above the horizontal" or "15° east of north").
Part D

Properties of vectors

1 · Vectors can be moved parallel to themselves in a diagram

Imagine a toy car crossing a moving walkway. Its velocity is the combination of two independent motions: 0.80 m/s across the walkway and 1.5 m/s along it. Because a vector only has a magnitude and a direction, you may slide it anywhere on the diagram as long as its length and direction do not change. That lets you draw one vector's tail at the tip of the other (the triangle or polygon method).

The resultant velocity of the car, found with a ruler and a protractor.

2 · Vectors can be added in any order

The sum of two or more vectors does not depend on the order of the addition: A + B = B + A. A marathon runner who makes the same four displacements in a different order ends up with the same total displacement.

3 · To subtract a vector, add its opposite

The negative of a vector has the same magnitude but the opposite direction. For example, the negative of 30 m/s to the west is −30 m/s to the west, which is +30 m/s to the east. Adding a vector to its negative gives zero.

A − B = A + (−B)

To subtract in two dimensions, first draw the negative of the vector to be subtracted, then add it with the triangle method.

4 · Multiplying or dividing a vector by a scalar gives a vector

A cab driver is told to go twice as fast. His velocity vcab is multiplied by the scalar 2: the result, 2vcab, is a vector with twice the magnitude and the same direction. If the driver is told to go twice as fast in the opposite direction, the vector is multiplied by −2: the result, −2vcab, has twice the magnitude and points the opposite way.

ScalarEffect on a vector v
2twice as long, same direction
½ (or ÷ 2)half as long, same direction
−1same length, opposite direction (the negative of v)
−2twice as long, opposite direction
Did you know?The word vector is also used by airline pilots and navigators: it is the particular path followed (or to be followed), given as a compass heading.

Remember when solving problems

  1. Analyze — is the quantity a vector? List magnitudes and directions; choose a scale.
  2. Plan — add vectors tail-to-head; to subtract, add the negative; apply scalars before adding.
  3. Solve — measure the resultant's length (× scale) and its angle from a stated reference line.
  4. Check — the resultant can never be longer than the sum of the lengths, and it must make sense in direction.
