Duties and rights · Human rights

Human Rights in the Family, School and Community

Guiding question

“What happens when a right is not respected, and what can I do about it?”

Learning target: I can name and classify rights from the Constitution (political, social and family), connect each right with a duty, and give a respectful, well-argued response to a case of violated rights.

Unit indicator: Shows respect for human rights in the family, school and community.  ·  Textbook: Creciendo en Valores 8, pp. 101–108

Rights and duties go together

A Constitution (the Magna Carta) is the fundamental law of a country. It organizes life in society and the work of the State, and it guarantees the people their rights and freedoms. In Nicaragua it sets the limits and relations between the powers of the State (Legislative, Executive, Electoral and Judicial) and between them and the citizens.

Human beings, just by existing, have the right to life: no one can be deprived of it for any reason. Everyone has the same rights, and everyone has the duty to respect the rights of others.

Three groups of rights in the Constitution

Political rights (Arts. 47–55)Take part in public life: vote and be elected, petition the authorities, assemble peacefully, join organizations and parties.
Social rights (Arts. 56–69)Work, education and culture, health, a healthy environment, social security, food, housing, sport and recreation, truthful information, freedom of religion.
Family rights (Arts. 70–79)Protection of the family, equal rights of all children, special protection for children, mothers and the elderly, equal responsibilities of parents, adoption in the child's interest.
