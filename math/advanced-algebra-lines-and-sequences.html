Parallel and Perpendicular Lines & Arithmetic Sequences

This page has two parts. In Part 1 you will use slopes to decide whether lines are parallel, perpendicular or neither, and to write equations of lines. In Part 2 you will work with arithmetic sequences, using recursive and explicit formulas. The two parts are connected: the points of an arithmetic sequence lie on a line whose slope is the common difference.

Objectives

  • Find slopes from points and from equations in different forms.
  • Decide whether two lines are parallel (equal slopes) or perpendicular (negative reciprocal slopes).
  • Write the equation of a line through a point that is parallel or perpendicular to a given line.
  • Recognize arithmetic sequences and find the common difference.
  • Write recursive and explicit formulas, convert between them, and find any term or the term number.

Part 1 — Parallel and perpendicular lines

PARALLEL

Parallel lines

  • Parallel lines are in the same plane and never meet. Non-vertical parallel lines have the same slope and different y-intercepts.
  • If the slopes are equal and the intercepts are equal, it is the same line (not parallel).
  • Any two vertical lines (such as x = 2 and x = −5) are parallel; any two horizontal lines are parallel.
m1 = m2
y = 2x + 1   y = 2x − 3
PERPENDICULAR

Perpendicular lines

  • Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle. Their slopes are negative reciprocals: the product of the slopes is −1.
  • To get the perpendicular slope: flip the fraction and change the sign (2 becomes −1/2; −3/4 becomes 4/3).
  • A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular.
m1 · m2 = −1    or    m2 = −1m1
y = 2x − 1   y = −(1/2)x + 3/2
Remember: opposite slopes (like 3 and −3) are not perpendicular; you also need the reciprocal.
METHOD

Writing the equation of a parallel or perpendicular line

  1. Find the slope m of the given line (rewrite as y = mx + b if needed).
  2. Choose the new slope: the same m for a parallel line, the negative reciprocal −1/m for a perpendicular line.
  3. Use point-slope form with the given point: y − y1 = mnew(x − x1).
  4. Simplify to y = mx + b (or any equivalent form).

Worked examples — lines

Example 1 — Are the lines parallel?

Are y = 3x − 2 and 6x − 2y = 8 parallel?

Solve the second for y: −2y = −6x + 8, so y = 3x − 4.

Both slopes are 3 and the intercepts (−2 and −4) differ: parallel.

Example 2 — Are the lines perpendicular?

Are y = −(2/3)x + 1 and 3x − 2y = 6 perpendicular?

Second line: −2y = −3x + 6, so y = (3/2)x − 3.

Slopes: −2/3 and 3/2. Product: (−2/3)(3/2) = −1, so the lines are perpendicular.

Example 3 — A parallel line through a point

Write the equation of the line through (4, −1) that is parallel to y = 2x + 5.

Slope: 2 (same as the given line). Point-slope: y − (−1) = 2(x − 4).

y + 1 = 2x − 8, so y = 2x − 9.

Example 4 — A perpendicular line through a point

Write the equation of the line through (−3, 2) that is perpendicular to 4x + 2y = 7.

4x + 2y = 7 gives y = −2x + 7/2, so the slope is −2. The perpendicular slope is 1/2.

y − 2 = (1/2)(x + 3), so y = (1/2)x + 3/2 + 2 = y = (1/2)x + 7/2.

Example 5 — Classifying lines from points

Line AB passes through A(1, 2) and B(4, 8); line CD passes through C(−2, −5) and D(0, −1). Parallel, perpendicular or neither?

Slope AB = (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 2. Slope CD = (−1 + 5)/(0 + 2) = 2.

The slopes are equal and C is not on line AB, so the lines are parallel.

Example 6 — Finding an unknown coefficient

For what values of k are kx + 3y = 6 and 2x − y = 5 (a) parallel? (b) perpendicular?

Slopes: −k/3 and 2.

(a) Parallel: −k/3 = 2, so k = −6.

(b) Perpendicular: (−k/3)(2) = −1, so 2k/3 = 1 and k = 3/2.

Part 2 — Arithmetic sequences

DEFINITION

What is an arithmetic sequence?

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers: a1, a2, a3, … In an arithmetic sequence you get each term by adding the same number, the common difference d, to the previous term.

d = an − an−1 the same for every pair of consecutive terms (positive: increasing; negative: decreasing; zero: constant)

Example: 7, 11, 15, 19, … has d = 4. Example: 20, 14, 8, 2, … has d = −6. The sequence 3, 6, 12, 24, … is not arithmetic (it multiplies).

FORMULAS

Recursive and explicit formulas

FormulaRuleGood for
Recursivea1 = (first term);  an = an−1 + ddescribing how the sequence grows; it needs the previous term
Explicitan = a1 + (n − 1)dfinding any term directly (for example a100)
  • A recursive formula always needs the first term as well as the rule.
  • The explicit formula can be simplified to an = dn + (a1 − d), a linear expression in n.
  • To find the term number of a value, set the explicit formula equal to that value and solve for n; n must be a positive whole number.
  • With two known terms ap and aq: d = (aq − ap)/(q − p).
CONNECTION

Arithmetic sequences and lines

The explicit formula an = dn + (a1 − d) has the same form as y = mx + b. The points (n, an) lie on a line whose slope is the common difference. Sequences with the same d give parallel lines.

Points of an = 4n + 3 (7, 11, 15, 19, 23): slope 4

Worked examples — sequences

Example 7 — Common difference, recursive and explicit formulas

Write the recursive and the explicit formula for 7, 11, 15, 19, … and find a20.

a1 = 7 and d = 11 − 7 = 4.

Recursive: a1 = 7;  an = an−1 + 4.

Explicit: an = 7 + (n − 1)(4) = 4n + 3.

a20 = 4(20) + 3 = 83.

Example 8 — Which term is it?

Which term of 2, 5, 8, 11, … equals 98? Is 100 a term?

d = 3, so an = 2 + 3(n − 1) = 3n − 1.

3n − 1 = 98 gives n = 33: 98 is the 33rd term.

3n − 1 = 100 gives n = 101/3 ≈ 33.67, not a whole number, so 100 is not a term.

Example 9 — From two terms

In an arithmetic sequence a5 = 17 and a9 = 29. Find d, a1 and the explicit formula.

From term 5 to term 9 there are 4 steps: 4d = 29 − 17 = 12, so d = 3.

a1 = a5 − 4d = 17 − 12 = 5.

an = 5 + 3(n − 1) = 3n + 2.

Example 10 — Converting between formulas

(a) Convert a1 = 12, an = an−1 − 4 to an explicit formula. (b) Convert an = 5n − 3 to a recursive formula.

(a) a1 = 12 and d = −4: an = 12 − 4(n − 1) = 16 − 4n.

(b) a1 = 5(1) − 3 = 2 and d = 5 (the coefficient of n): a1 = 2;  an = an−1 + 5.

Example 11 — A word problem

The first rung of a ladder is 30 cm above the ground and the rungs are 28 cm apart. (a) Write an explicit formula for the height of rung n. (b) How high is the 10th rung?

(a) a1 = 30, d = 28: an = 30 + 28(n − 1) = 28n + 2.

(b) a10 = 28(10) + 2 = 282 cm.
