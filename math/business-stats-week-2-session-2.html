Mean vs median and skew

Right skew: mean > median. Left skew: mean < median. Symmetric: mean ≈ median. Reporting both tells the reader about the shape.

Excel

=AVERAGE(A2:A100), =MEDIAN(…), =MODE.SNGL(…), weighted mean =SUMPRODUCT(w, x)/SUM(w), geometric mean =GEOMEAN(factors).

Measures of variation

Two stores can have the same mean daily sales yet very different reliability. Spread measures that variability.

Variation measures
MeasureFormulaNotes
Rangemax − minUses only two values; very sensitive to outliers

Sample variances² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)Units are squared
Sample std devs = √s²Typical distance from the mean; same units as data
Population std devσ = √[Σ(x − μ)² / N]Use only if you have the whole population
Coefficient of variationCV = s / x̄ × 100 %Compares spread across different units or scales
Why n − 1?

Deviations are measured from the sample mean, which is by construction “closer” to the sample points than the true mean is. Dividing by n − 1 corrects this and makes s² an unbiased estimate of σ².

Worked example · sample standard deviation by hand

Weekly returns (units) of five stores: 4, 8, 6, 5, 7. Find s.

  1. Mean = 30/5 = 6.
  2. Deviations from 6: −2, 2, 0, −1, 1 (they sum to 0).
  3. Squares: 4, 4, 0, 1, 1; sum = 10.
  4. s² = 10/(5 − 1) = 2.5; s = √2.5 = 1.581.
  5. (If these five were the whole population: σ² = 10/5 = 2, σ = 1.414.)
  6. CV = 1.581/6 × 100 % = 26.4 %.
Answer: s² = 2.5, s = 1.581 units, CV ≈ 26.4 %.

Using the standard deviation

  • Empirical rule (bell-shaped data): about 68 % within ±1 s, 95 % within ±2 s, 99.7 % within ±3 s.
  • Chebyshev (any shape): at least 1 − 1/k² within ±k s. So ≥ 75 % within 2 s and ≥ 88.9 % within 3 s.
  • A value more than 2–3 standard deviations from the mean deserves a second look.
Excel

=STDEV.S(range) (sample), =STDEV.P(range) (population), =VAR.S, =VAR.P. Using STDEV.P on a sample understates the variability.

Percentiles and quartiles

The p-th percentile is a value with about p % of the data at or below it. Quartiles split the ordered data into four parts: Q1 = 25th percentile, Q2 = median = 50th, Q3 = 75th.

L = p × (n + 1)Position of the p-th percentile in the sorted list (p as a decimal). If L is not a whole number, interpolate between neighbouring values.
  1. Sort the data from smallest to largest.
  2. Compute L = p(n + 1).
  3. If L is a whole number, take that value. Otherwise go L’s fractional part of the way from the value below to the value above.
Methods differ slightly

Textbooks and software use several conventions. The rule above matches Excel’s QUARTILE.EXC / PERCENTILE.EXC. Excel’s older default QUARTILE.INC uses L = 1 + p(n − 1) and can differ in the last digit for small data sets. Use the method in your lecturer’s slides and state it. The practice questions here use L = p(n + 1).

Percentile rank of a value: the percentage of observations below it, roughly (number below / n) × 100.

Five-number summary, boxplots and outliers

The five-number summary is Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max. A boxplot draws it: a box from Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median and whiskers to the most extreme values that are not outliers.

lower fence = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQRValues outside the fences are plotted as individual points (potential outliers)

Worked example · summary and outliers

Daily sales ($100s) on 11 days: 12, 14, 15, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 45.

  1. Sorted already; n = 11; Min = 12, Max = 45.
  2. Median: L = 0.5 × 12 = 6 → 6th value = 17.
  3. Q1: L = 0.25 × 12 = 3 → 3rd value = 15. Q3: L = 0.75 × 12 = 9 → 9th value = 21. IQR = 6.
  4. Fences: 15 − 1.5·6 = 6 and 21 + 1.5·6 = 30.
  5. 45 > 30 → outlier. The upper whisker stops at 22, the largest value inside the fences.
  6. Mean = 19.45, s = 8.98: both are inflated by the 45.
Answer: Five-number summary 12, 15, 17, 21, 45; one outlier (45).
  • Box in the middle and whiskers about equal: roughly symmetric.
  • Median closer to Q1 with a long upper whisker or outliers: right-skewed.
  • Always investigate an outlier: error, special event, or genuine extreme customer. Do not delete it just because it is inconvenient.

The takeaway

The mean, standard deviation and range are not resistant: one extreme value moves them a lot. The median and IQR are resistant. For skewed data or data with outliers, report the median and IQR (and look at a boxplot). For roughly symmetric data without outliers, the mean and standard deviation are efficient summaries.

Common mistakes

  • Using STDEV.P (÷ N) for a sample; use STDEV.S (÷ n − 1).
  • Forgetting to sort before finding a median or quartile.
  • Averaging percentages or growth rates arithmetically (use a weighted or geometric mean).
  • Reporting the mean of a skewed variable (income) as “typical”.
  • Deleting outliers automatically; investigate first.
  • Comparing standard deviations of variables on different scales without the CV.
